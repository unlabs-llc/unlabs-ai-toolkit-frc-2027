# Licensing FAQ

Short version: **if you are a team, a school, or a nonprofit, it is free. If you sell something built
on it, it is $1,000 per competition year — bought outright, no subscription.**

This FAQ is a plain-language guide, not the license. The [PolyForm Noncommercial License
1.0.0](./LICENSE.md) and the [commercial license](./COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md) are the actual terms, and
they control if anything here reads differently.

## Do I need to pay?

### I'm on an FRC team. Do I need to pay?

**No.** Team use is free — whether your team is a school club, a 501(c)(3) booster organization, or an
unincorporated group of students and parents. Use the skills, change them, share them with other
teams. Keep the copyright notice with anything you pass along.

### I'm a teacher / my school wants to use these in class. Do we need to pay?

**No.** Schools, colleges, and universities are covered by the free license. So are libraries, public
research organizations, and government bodies.

### We're a nonprofit and we charge tuition for our robotics camp. Do we need to pay?

**No.** The free license covers charitable organizations and educational institutions *regardless of
where their funding comes from*. Charging tuition doesn't change that.

### I run a for-profit robotics camp / training company / consultancy. Do I need to pay?

**Yes**, if what you sell is built on these skills. See the next section for what a license covers.

### I'm an individual. Can I use these for my own projects?

**Yes**, free — for learning, experimenting, hobby projects, and personal study. If you start selling
something built on them, you need a commercial license.

### Will you ask me to prove I qualify for the free license?

**Possibly.** Anyone claiming a license bears the burden of showing they have one. If it isn't obvious
that you fall into one of the free categories and we ask, we expect you to be able to show it —
nonprofit registration, school or district affiliation, team number and affiliation. If you can't,
we'll treat the use as commercial, and the 32-day notice-and-cure process applies before anything
else happens.

## How the commercial license works

### What does $1,000 actually buy?

**One Release, for one competition year, for your whole organization.** Each FRC competition year gets
its own Release of the skills. Your license covers the Release named in your order confirmation.

### Is that a twelve-month subscription?

**No.** Your term runs from the day you pay until the end of that competition year. The fee buys a
Release, not a fixed number of months.

### What's a "competition year"?

An FRC season as FIRST designates it — the "2027 season," for example. It runs from FIRST's Kickoff
for that season to the close of FIRST's world championship event for that season. FIRST sets those
dates, so a competition year isn't a calendar year and its length can vary.

### I licensed the 2027 Release. Do I get the 2028 Release?

**Only if you buy it.** The 2028 Release is a separate Release even though it carries material forward
from 2027, and it's a separate purchase at whatever price is published then.

### Does it auto-renew?

**No.** Nothing renews, nothing cancels, and we don't keep a payment method on file. When you want the
next competition year's Release, buy it. That's the whole mechanism.

### When can I buy a license for next season?

**A competition year's license goes on sale when the previous competition year ends** — that is, once
that season's world championship event closes. You don't have to wait for the new Release to exist.

*Worked example.* The 2027 season closes and you want to run preparation classes over the summer. The
2028 Release doesn't exist yet. You buy the **2028** license as soon as it goes on sale, use the 2027
skills over the summer at no extra charge, and move to the 2028 Release when it comes out — same fee,
one purchase.

### What if you don't publish a Release next year?

**Then there's nothing to buy, and you owe nothing.** We don't promise to publish a Release for any
competition year. You only ever pay for one you choose to buy.

And if you already bought a license for a Release we never publish, **your license keeps covering the
earlier Release for the rest of your term** — you are not left with nothing.

### What happens to what I already shipped if my license lapses?

**It stays licensed, permanently.** Anything you distributed while your license was current stays
covered forever, and your customers are unaffected. What you can't do after your license ends is
distribute anything *new* commercially — including new copies of the Release you used to hold.

## Everything else

### Can I fork the repo and publish my changes?

**Yes**, for noncommercial purposes. Pass along the license terms (or the URL) and the copyright
notice with what you publish. If your fork is part of something you sell, you need a commercial
license.

### Can I use the AI output in our robot code / our competition submission?

**Yes.** The license covers the skills themselves — the instruction files. What you produce using them
is yours to use. But please read the next answer.

### Is any of this safety-checked or competition-legal?

**No.** These skills steer a third-party AI model that Unlabs doesn't control. Output can be wrong,
incomplete, or unsuitable. **Nothing here is engineering, safety, inspection, or professional advice**,
and none of it should be relied on to decide whether a robot, part, or practice is safe or
competition-legal. Check the rules and check the work.

### Is this open source?

**No** — and we don't call it that. PolyForm Noncommercial is a *source-available* license: you can
read and modify the code, but it restricts commercial use, so it isn't open source under the Open
Source Initiative or Free Software Foundation definitions. We think that's the honest description.

### Can I use the Unlabs AI Toolkit for FRC 2027 Season Teams or Unlabs name for my product?

**No.** Neither license grants any trademark rights. You can accurately say your product is built on
the Unlabs AI Toolkit for FRC 2027 Season Teams skills — that's it.

And separately: **FIRST®, FIRST Robotics Competition, and FRC® are FIRST's trademarks, not ours.** We
have no authority over them and can't grant you anything in them. If you're building a commercial
offering aimed at FRC teams, how you may name and market it is between you and FIRST.

### What if I've been using it commercially without a license?

Get in touch. The free license gives a **32-day cure period** after written notice — come into
compliance in that window and your rights continue. We would rather sell you a license than chase you.
